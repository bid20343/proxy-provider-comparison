# webshare vs iproyal: Compare proxy types, pricing models, and the right fit for scraping or long-lived accounts

Choosing between Webshare and IPRoyal gets confusing when the headline prices look wildly different. One provider advertises free proxies and very cheap bulk datacenter plans; the other sells mobile proxies, non-expiring residential traffic, and longer sticky sessions. Those are not interchangeable products, even when both appear in the same “cheap proxy” search.

The practical question is simpler: **what kind of IP do you need, how long must it remain stable, and will unused traffic become wasted budget?**

For legal tasks such as price monitoring, market research, SEO checks, ad verification, and authorized data collection, Webshare is generally easier to test and cheaper at entry level. IPRoyal is the more flexible choice when mobile IPs, persistent sessions, or non-expiring residential bandwidth matter. If your work mainly needs stable U.S. ISP IPs with unlimited bandwidth rather than rotating traffic, HypeProxies is another route worth considering.

## The short answer: which provider wins?

Here is the useful version before getting buried in proxy terminology:

- **Choose Webshare** when you want a free starting point, low-cost datacenter proxies in volume, ZIP/ASN targeting for residential traffic, or a simple self-service setup.
- **Choose IPRoyal** when you specifically need mobile proxies, residential bandwidth that does not expire, pay-as-you-go flexibility, or sticky sessions that can last longer.
- **Consider HypeProxies** when rotating residential bandwidth is the wrong billing model for your project and you need fixed U.S. ISP IPs with unlimited bandwidth.

Neither Webshare nor IPRoyal is automatically “better.” A $3.50-per-GB rotating residential plan can be economical for one-off collection jobs, while a stable monthly ISP allocation makes more sense for ongoing account-based work. Buying the wrong proxy type is usually more expensive than choosing a provider with a slightly higher sticker price.

> A low per-IP price is only a bargain if that IP type fits the target, location, session duration, and bandwidth pattern of your project.

## The first decision: rotating, static, datacenter, or mobile?

Most Webshare vs IPRoyal comparisons become clearer once you stop comparing brand names and start comparing proxy categories.

### Rotating residential proxies

Rotating residential proxies route requests through consumer IP addresses and change the IP on a schedule or by session configuration. They are commonly used for permitted web-data collection, SERP monitoring, regional content checks, and large research jobs where repeatedly using one address would be unhelpful.

Webshare lists rotating residential plans from **$3.50 per GB for 1 GB** on its monthly pricing page, with lower per-GB rates at higher volume. IPRoyal’s residential offering supports subscription and pay-as-you-go purchasing; its pricing page presents volume-based per-GB rates, and its residential traffic is designed not to expire.

This produces a real cost difference:

- If you consume the bandwidth predictably every month, Webshare’s discounted monthly residential pricing can be attractive.
- If your project runs in bursts, IPRoyal’s non-expiring traffic can reduce the chance of paying for bandwidth that disappears at renewal.
- If your task needs a stable identity for several days, the session limit matters as much as the GB price.

Do not buy 100 GB because its unit price looks better unless you can genuinely use it. A cheaper rate per GB is not helpful when half the plan remains unused.

### Datacenter proxies

Datacenter proxies usually prioritize speed, volume, and price. They are useful for targets that permit automation or do not require a residential IP reputation. Typical legitimate uses include monitoring your own infrastructure, retrieving public product pages where terms allow it, and high-volume tasks that do not need a persistent consumer-looking address.

Webshare is particularly aggressive here. Its public pricing shows:

- **10 free proxies** on the free plan;
- **100 proxy servers for $2.99 per month**;
- lower per-proxy pricing as volume increases.

The free tier makes Webshare practical for setup checks, testing an integration, or small personal projects. It also creates a low-friction way to see whether datacenter IPs are actually suitable before buying a larger allocation.

IPRoyal’s datacenter plans use a different framing: pricing starts from a per-proxy rate and depends on the selected lease period. That can work well when you want dedicated IPs for a defined term and do not need hundreds or thousands of addresses.

### Static residential and ISP proxies

“Static residential” and “ISP proxy” are often used closely, though providers may label them differently. These are fixed IPs associated with ISP networks rather than a constantly rotating pool. They suit workflows that need one stable address over time, such as authorized account management, localization checks, ecommerce monitoring, and repeated access to services where a session needs consistency.

Webshare’s static residential plans start at **20 proxies for $6 per month**, with lower unit pricing at higher quantities. IPRoyal also provides ISP and static residential products, with plans sold per proxy and according to the selected duration.

The important detail is bandwidth. Before comparing per-IP cost, check whether a plan has an allowance, a fair-use condition, or unlimited bandwidth. A low monthly IP fee paired with a restrictive data cap can be a poor fit for a busy collection workflow.

### Mobile proxies

This is where the comparison ends quickly: **IPRoyal offers mobile proxies; Webshare does not.**

Mobile proxies use IPs from cellular networks. They are usually more expensive and should not be purchased simply because “mobile” sounds premium. They can be relevant when a legitimate use case requires mobile-network routing or a carrier-associated IP reputation.

IPRoyal’s public mobile pricing begins with plans such as 2 GB, 10 GB, and 50 GB, with the per-GB rate falling as traffic volume rises. Its mobile offering starts from a monthly minimum rather than a free or tiny trial-style allocation.

If you do not specifically need mobile IPs, avoid paying mobile-proxy prices for a job that a stable ISP proxy or rotating residential endpoint can handle.

## Sticky sessions and IP persistence: a detail that changes the answer

A sticky session keeps the same proxy IP for a defined period. This can matter when a workflow has a login, a cart, a region-specific browsing sequence, or another legitimate process that should not suddenly appear from a different country halfway through.

IPRoyal supports sticky residential sessions for up to **seven days**. Webshare caps sticky sessions at **24 hours**.

That difference is not cosmetic:

- A one-hour price-monitoring run probably does not care.
- A multi-day workflow that must retain the same address may find Webshare’s limit restrictive.
- A stable ISP or static residential proxy can be more straightforward than using sticky rotating traffic if the job requires a predictable fixed IP.

## Questions to answer before buying any proxy plan

A little preparation prevents most bad proxy purchases. Write down the answers to these questions first:

1. **Does the target allow your intended activity?**
   Proxies do not override a website’s terms, access controls, or legal restrictions. Use them only for authorized, compliant work.

2. **Do you need a fixed IP or rotating IPs?**
   Fixed work points toward ISP/static residential proxies. Broad distributed collection often points toward rotating residential proxies.

3. **Do you need mobile-network IPs?**
   If yes, Webshare is out of the running. Start with IPRoyal’s mobile product or another mobile-focused provider.

4. **Will you use bandwidth every month?**
   Predictable monthly consumption can suit Webshare. Irregular consumption makes IPRoyal’s non-expiring residential traffic more appealing.

5. **Which locations are mandatory?**
   Country-level coverage is not enough if you need a specific city, ZIP code, ASN, or U.S. state. Check live availability before committing.

6. **How long must a session remain on one IP?**
   A one-day sticky-session ceiling and a seven-day ceiling lead to different product choices.

7. **Is bandwidth or IP count the real cost driver?**
   For a high-bandwidth fixed-IP workflow, an unlimited-bandwidth ISP plan can be easier to budget than per-GB rotating residential traffic.
